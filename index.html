Fuelling

The calories went up, and that is correct. About 2,500 kcal/week of running was just added to the system. Eating 2,450 now would be a ~950 kcal/day deficit — that pace strips muscle, tanks quality-run performance, and is how people get injured 10 weeks into a block.

The deficit is in the food relative to the new workload, not in the raw number. Weekly-average TDEE is ~3,395; eating ~2,850 is a ~555 kcal/day deficit ≈ 1.1 lb/week.

Calorie cycling — eat to match the day. Protein (215 g) and fat (80 g) stay constant every day. Only carbs move.

This is optional — hitting 2,850 flat every day works fine and is simpler. Cycling just puts the fuel where the work is.

Recalibration. Bodyweight is an input, not a constant. Every 10 lb lost, subtract ~50 kcal from baseline. As Runna builds mileage toward the race (likely 25–30 mi/week by November), add roughly +140 kcal per extra weekly mile ÷ 7 — around +250 kcal/day at peak mileage. Holding 2,850 through a 30 mi/week block would be under-fuelling.

Adjust on the 4-week trend, not the scale on any given morning. Glycogen and fluid shifts from a long run can swing weight 3–4 lb overnight and mean nothing. Do not re-derive the baseline from step counts — the formula's error (~±300 kcal) is wider than the gap between a 10k and a 15k step day.

Progression to race day

Base (now → ~Oct 10): full program as written. Add weight or 1 rep per week on the top compound of each day.

Build (~Oct 10 → Nov 14): hold intensity, cut accessories from 3 sets to 2 as mileage climbs. Keep the soleus work at full volume — it is injury insurance.

Taper (final 2–3 weeks): 2 sets across the board, and cut Bulgarian split squats entirely. Never approach failure.

When run volume and lifting volume conflict in the last month, the running wins — that's the event being trained for.
